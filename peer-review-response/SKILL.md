# Peer Review Response

## Why this matters

The response letter is itself evaluated — editors and reviewers judge not
just whether the science changed but whether every point was taken
seriously. A dismissive, defensive, or incomplete response is one of the
fastest ways to turn a "minor revision" into a rejection on resubmission,
independent of whether the underlying science was actually fine.

## Process

1. **Address every point, without exception.** Reviewers and editors check
   for completeness. Skipping a comment, even a minor one, reads as either
   sloppiness or evasion. If a request genuinely can't be done (data
   doesn't exist, out of scope, prohibitively expensive), say so explicitly
   with reasoning rather than ignoring it.

2. **Separate what changed in the manuscript from what didn't**, and make
   that distinction obvious to the editor, who is often skimming for
   "were the changes made." For each point:
   - Quote or closely paraphrase the reviewer's comment.
   - State what was done in response (and where — page/line/section).
   - If nothing was changed, explain why the original approach still
     stands, respectfully and with evidence/reasoning, not just assertion.

3. **Distinguish comments that require new analysis/data from those that
   are about clarity/presentation.** The latter are usually straightforward
   to resolve; the former need real engineering/analysis work before the
   letter can be drafted — don't promise a result in the letter before
   confirming what the actual reanalysis shows.

4. **When you disagree with a reviewer, argue with evidence, not
   defensiveness.** "We respectfully disagree" needs to be followed by a
   substantive reason (a citation, a clarification of what was
   misunderstood, an explanation of why the suggested alternative doesn't
   apply here) — not just a restatement of the original claim. Reviewers
   who feel unheard tend to dig in on resubmission; reviewers who see their
   point taken seriously (even when not fully adopted) usually don't.

5. **If two reviewers conflict**, don't silently pick one — acknowledge
   the tension in the response and explain the reasoning for the choice
   made, ideally to both reviewers' comments in their respective sections.

## Tone

- Professional, appreciative of the review effort, non-defensive — even
  when a comment feels unfair or reflects a misunderstanding, the response
  should read as engaging with the point, not scoring rhetorical points
  against the reviewer.
- Avoid over-apologizing or excessive hedging — a response that reads as
  anxious rather than confident undermines trust in the results as much as
  a defensive one does.
- Never characterize a reviewer's comment as wrong without substantiating
  why; "the reviewer is mistaken" without evidence reads as arrogance, and
  editors notice.

## Structure of the response letter

Use this format unless the journal specifies its own:

```
We thank the reviewers and editor for their thoughtful comments, which
have substantially improved the manuscript. Below, we address each point
in turn; reviewer comments are in italics/quoted, and our responses
follow. Changes in the manuscript are indicated in [color/tracked
changes/line numbers] as requested.

Reviewer 1

Comment 1: [quoted/paraphrased comment]
Response: [what was done, or the reasoning if nothing was changed;
reference to the specific location of the change in the revised
manuscript]

Comment 2: ...

Reviewer 2
...

Editor
...
```

## Before sending

- Check every reviewer comment against the response list — nothing
  silently dropped.
- Check that every "we have revised X" claim actually corresponds to a
  real change in the manuscript, with an accurate location reference —
  claiming a change that wasn't actually made is caught easily and damages
  credibility badly.
- If substantial new analysis was added in response to a comment, make
  sure it's consistent with the `data-analysis` skill's standards
  (correct method, honest reporting) before describing it in the letter.
- Read the full response letter in one pass for tone — a letter drafted
  comment-by-comment can drift defensive by the end even if it started
  measured; smooth that out.
